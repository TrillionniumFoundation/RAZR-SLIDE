## Mathematical boundary

The inclusion proof needs one complete exterior channel for the cylindrical
material and one complete exterior channel for the other material's slab.
Searching all twelve channels is an algorithmic choice. Every selected
channel must still reach the same original known-exterior endpoint and
account for every original boundary piece for the complete original D.
The prefix may suggest a candidate but cannot impose a material restriction.

The prepared producer scans each original ordered channel until the first
unresolved piece. A channel with no unresolved piece is already a full
census. Otherwise the first unknown cylinder/plane pieces only rank possible
pairs. At most two pairs are tried. Their scans resume at `len(codes)`, so
previous prefix visits are neither discarded nor counted as free repeated
work. Each new visit increments the original piece counter. All original
indices remain in order, and full selected-channel observations contain the
entire source-piece census.

After completing a candidate pair, the producer reconstructs the cylinder
from every unresolved piece, rechecks its full-channel/full-D monotonicity,
and recomputes the slab projection over every unresolved piece. It does not
reuse the optimistic prefix bound as a proof. An unsupported or ineffective
pair leaves the whole positive-volume box unresolved. A poor selection can
lose performance or tightness; it cannot justify deleting material.

The prepared checker verifies original exterior endpoints, all actually
visited piece proofs, complete original shifts, and prefix/complete census
labels. It specifically requires both selected channels to be complete,
then independently verifies their full cylinder/slab restrictions. Unselected
prefixes contribute to accounting, not to the volume bound. A result marked
as unresolved must retain the full box measure. The original 4096 global
splits, depth 24, 12000000 pieces and 600-second/805306368-byte limits remain
in the full driver and reader.
